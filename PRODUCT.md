# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Math Bowl Practice serves elementary students in grades 1-5 who are preparing for Mental Math Bowl competition problems, plus parents and teachers who coach those students. Students use the app to build speed, accuracy, and confidence through focused practice. Parents and teachers use it to understand progress, spot weak categories, print practice worksheets, and guide next-step coaching.

## Product Purpose

The product helps students get competition-ready through short randomized quizzes, mental math strategy support, optional timing pressure, printable worksheets, and progress tracking. Success means students can practice independently, see what they missed, learn a better mental move, and return stronger; coaches can quickly understand where help is needed without digging through noisy details.

## Brand Personality

Encouraging, focused, game-day-ready. The interface should feel supportive without becoming soft or babyish, polished without becoming corporate, and energetic without turning into an arcade.

## Anti-references

Avoid babyish classroom visuals, sterile enterprise dashboards, flashy arcade-game treatment, generic AI tutor aesthetics, and card-heavy layouts where every element has the same weight. Avoid decoration that distracts from practice, coaching, timing, or progress.

## Design Principles

Practice should feel purposeful: every screen should make the next useful action obvious.

Balance student confidence with coach trust: playful practice moments can coexist with credible dashboard reporting.

Competition readiness comes from clarity under pressure: timers, results, and corrections should reduce uncertainty, not add noise.

Teach the next move, not just the score: missed problems should point students toward a mental math strategy or focused follow-up.

Respect repeated use: layouts should be fast to scan, predictable, and calm enough for frequent practice sessions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Preserve strong keyboard navigation, visible focus states, readable contrast, and reduced-motion support. Do not rely on color alone for correctness, warning, success, or category meaning; pair color with text, icons, shape, or position so students with color vision differences can still understand feedback.
